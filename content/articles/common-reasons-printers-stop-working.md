We see more printers than you might expect for a computer support business, and the honest truth is that most printer problems come from the same short list of causes. Work through it in order and you will either fix it yourself or know exactly what to tell us when you call.

## 1. The connection, not the printer

By far the most common "printer is broken" call ends up being a connection problem. The router power-cycled itself overnight and gave out new IP addresses, the printer dropped off the WiFi by its own logic, or someone unplugged the USB cable to vacuum behind the desk.

Try this first: print from a different device. If the laptop that has never printed wirelessly works but the PC that always did does not, you are looking at the printer's network connection rather than the printer itself.

## 2. Driver confusion

A printer driver is small software on the computer that knows how to talk to your model. Windows Update, a reinstall or a new printer model can leave two drivers stacked up, so jobs go to a printer that no longer exists.

If "Add Printer" appears twice with slightly different names, delete both and start again. It is tedious the first time but almost always ends in a working printer.

## 3. The stuck spooler

If jobs stack up and refuse to shift, or the printer prints a blank page and grumbles, the spooler (the Windows service that queues print jobs) has tripped. Restarting the "Print Spooler" service, or simply rebooting the printer and the computer in that order, clears it more often than not.

## 4. Consumables that are not what they say

Ink and toner levels are estimates, not facts. We regularly open machines where "full" cartridges have dried out after months of idle time, or where the soffixes have been refilled in a way the print head dislikes. If colours carry faint rings of damage, print a nozzle check and clean the heads. If nothing shifts after two cleans, the cartridge is often the culprit, not the printer.

## 5. Paper path issues

Chewed corners, rolled edges and repeated jams usually trace back to a single bit of torn paper lodged where you cannot see it, or to the rollers drying out with age. Open every door the printer offers and look with a torch. A single small scrap lodged at a roller will hammer the machine for months.

## 6. Sleep and standby quirks

Modern printers love deep sleep, and some will not come back from it politely. If the printer only fails after long idle, switch "deep sleep" off in its settings menu. It costs an extra few pence of electricity a year and a lot less frustration.
