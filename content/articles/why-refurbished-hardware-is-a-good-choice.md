When a laptop dies, the first reaction is usually "buy new". Sometimes that is right - but surprisingly often, a properly refurbished machine will do the same job for a fraction of the price. Here is what "properly" means, and why we are happy to sell our own checked refurb equipment to customers we will see again next year.

## What refurbished actually should mean

There is a big gap between the geezer on a marketplace selling old laptops out of a garage with no questions asked, and a machine genuinely sold as refurbished. We work to: a full wipe of the drive to UK standards, deep cleaning and thermal work, battery and component checks, and installation of a licensed operating system with the latest updates and a working antivirus. Anything without that is second-hand, not refurbished.

## Why data hygiene matters most

Used machines carry used data. A discarded office laptop can contain company accounts, customer contact lists and unencrypted files. If a supplier does not wipe the drive in front of you (or hand you a copy of a wiping certificate), do not buy from them. Everything we sell is sanitised both ways: we clean out whatever was on there, and we protect you from the previous owner.

## The economics that make sense

For a child's homework machine, a spare laptop for visitors, a shop till or a "just gets me on email" PC, you do not need the fastest chip of the year. A mid-spec business machine from three or four years ago is a better build and easier to repair, because parts are cheap and they were never glued shut in the first place.

You also lose far less if it gets dropped at a school bag or lost in a shoe shop. People treat new machines differently because each bump feels like a stabbing expense.

## When refurbished is not the right choice

If you are rendering full HD video, playing the newest titles, or running a many-vendor coin site or similar demanding setup in a customer-facing space, buy new: you want the top of what the market has for your money and the replacement warranty. Also, if you are running a business that legally needs a hardware standard, that document usually says "new" for a reason - and it is right, in that context.

Everything else is negotiable. And if you are not sure which you need, ask us: we sell re-fitted machines because we have checked the parts ourselves, and we are happy to talk you into one only when it is the sensible option. If it is not, we will say so and you can buy with confidence whichever way you jump.
