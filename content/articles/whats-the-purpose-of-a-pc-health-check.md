Most computers do not fail without warning - they whisper first. Storage creeps full, updates fall behind by months, a drive starts throwing the odd error. A health check exists to catch those whispers before they turn into the 7am panic "it won't start and I have a client at 9".

## What we actually look at

A proper health check is a checklist, not a vibe. Ours covers:

- **Storage** - how full the drive is, and how fast it is filling. Drives over about 90% full slow down dramatically and age faster.
- **Drive health** - a read of the drive's own self-monitoring data, which gives you weeks of warning before a disk dies outright.
- **Backups** - not just "is there a backup", but "does the recent restore actually work?" A backup that has never been tested is a hope, not a plan.
- **Updates** - Windows behind by months means unpatched security holes; that is the single most common opening that nasties use.
- **Antivirus** - running, current, and not clashing with something else someone installed quietly.
- **Bloat and leftover junk** - the toolbars, trial software and duplicate protection suits that slow the machine down a little every month.
- **Battery and cooling** for laptops - tired fans and clogged dust shorten a machine's life quietly.

## What a health check is NOT

It is not a sales pitch dressed up as engineering. If we find nothing alarming we tell you so, invoice for the time, and see you again in a few months. If we find something that can wait, we will say that too, and say why. You get the checklist findings in plain English, not acronyms.

## How often does it need doing?

For a family machine, once or twice a year is plenty. For a business - particularly any business that comes to a standstill without its computers - every quarter is the sensible answer, because a single laptop failure can cost more time than the checks cost money.

## The habit that prevents the most damage

If you take one piece from this article: know whether your backups are current. That single answer decides how bad a failed drive or a virus is going to be. It is an answer we ask before most others - and it is the thing a health check makes boringly reliable.
